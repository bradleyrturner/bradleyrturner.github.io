<span class="jm-badge">On the Job Market · 2026–2027</span>

I am a doctoral candidate in Economic Sociology at the MIT Sloan School of Management. I study identity and narrative in markets: how organizations, entrepreneurs, and innovators communicate strategically to persuade and coordinate stakeholders, and with what implications for markets and policy.

My dissertation centers on activist short sellers, investors who publish narrative reports of imminent decline alleging misconduct, fraud, and overvaluation. Drawing on organization theory and economic sociology, I show how outsiders use identity and narrative to build credibility and influence without being absorbed by the institutions they contest. In new work, I study how investors, founders, and policymakers destigmatized defense and dual-use technology, turning it into one of the fastest-growing categories in venture capital. The project compares the US, Canada, and Europe, and fits into a larger research stream on entrepreneurship, early-stage investing, and innovation policy. My job market paper is under revise-and-resubmit at *Research Policy*, and my review of narrative research appears in the *Academy of Management Annals* (2025).

My research investigates:

* How activist short sellers turn an outsider identity into a resource
* How strategic communicators tailor narratives for audiences with divergent standards
* When anecdotes strengthen or undermine founders' credibility with investors
* How investors, founders, and policymakers destigmatized defense and dual-use technology

Before MIT, I was awarded a Fulbright Fellowship, worked at Bridgewater Associates and Moody's, and managed a lab at Cornell Johnson. I hold an M.A. in Sociology from the University of Chicago and a B.S. in Economics from Syracuse University.

<span class="jm-badge">On the Job Market · 2026–2027</span>

I am a doctoral candidate in Economic Sociology at the MIT Sloan School of Management. I study identity and narrative in markets: how organizations, entrepreneurs, and activist investors communicate strategically to persuade and coordinate stakeholders. My solo-authored review of narrative research appears in the *Academy of Management Annals* (2025). My dissertation examines how activist short sellers selectively cultivate an outsider identity and craft rhetorical fit to bring new information to the market.

Before MIT, I was awarded a Fulbright Fellowship, worked at Bridgewater Associates and Moody's, and managed a lab at Cornell Johnson. I hold an M.A. in Sociology from the University of Chicago and a B.S. in Economics from Syracuse University.
